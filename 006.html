    Description

    Spider-Man represents one of the most complex cases to analyze, as it does not identify with a single individual, but acts as an infrastructural device, a functional "shell", that can be activated by different Original Cores. If Peter Parker is the biographical subject historically associated with the first activation of the system, the modular nature of the latter allows different individuals to "wear" the figure of Spider-Man, each bringing their own core of values, body, and personality into a predefined representative structure. This distinction between the "engine" (the individual) and the "bodywork" (the architecture) is what makes this case analytically fertile for observing dynamics of module substitution and adaptation.

    Starting from the analysis of the Spider-Man system as an autonomous entity, it configures itself as a quasi-subject based on three constant pillars: an alias module (the name Spider-Man), an avatar module (the full mask and arachnid costume), and an alter ego module (the heroic posture and sense of responsibility). When this system is inhabited by the original core of Peter Parker, the architecture stabilizes into a classical configuration where the representative module functions as a total shield to protect the subject's civil sphere. In this case, the system serves to manage the weight of trauma and guilt through a mask that enables a freedom of action and language, namely tactical irony, otherwise precluded to the Parker core.

    However, the effectiveness of the system emerges with clarity when the original core changes, as in the case of Miles Morales. Here, a different individual, with his own name, his own body, and his own urban aesthetic, "wears" the Spider-Man architecture. The system responds by adapting its modules: the avatar undergoes a chromatic renegotiation (black and red) and the tectonic module expands with new biological functionalities, but the public identity remains coherent thanks to the stability of the alias. Miles does not "is" Spider-Man in the ontological sense, but inhabits the Spider-Man architecture, demonstrating how the system functions as an interface capable of mediating between the subject's specific biography and the universal iconography of the spider.

    Similarly, the case of Gwen Stacy (Ghost-Spider) shows a decomposition where the female original core reprograms the avatar's silhouette through the introduction of a hood and an alternative chromatic palette. Here the architecture becomes a means of conveying a message of autonomy and identity search, diverging from the Parker model while keeping intact the logic of shielding and sacrifice. Even more extreme is the case of Otto Octavius in Superior Spider-Man: here the Spider-Man architecture is "hacked" by an antagonistic original core. Although the avatar remains almost unchanged to guarantee perceptual continuity in the eyes of the public, the alter ego module is completely rewritten by a utilitarian morality, demonstrating that the system can function even when the internal "pilot" is in total dissonance with the original values of the structure.

    In summary, Spider-Man must be understood as a complex and transpersonal architecture that cyclically evolves and stabilizes depending on who activates it. The continuity of the system is not determined by the biographical individual, but by the persistence of the alias and avatar modules, which guarantee absolute public recognizability. The individual, whether Parker, Morales, or O'Hara, provides energy and direction to the system, but it is the architecture itself that governs the relations of visibility and anonymity, making Spider-Man a methodologically powerful example of how a designed identity can survive and transcend the physical limits of the subject who wears it.

    Architecture
    Description

    The figure of Davide Mattei manifests as a modular system of extreme compactness and coherence, structured to operate a systematic shielding of the original identity through a sophisticated personal branding device that transforms the subject into a purely visual and narrative organism.

    At the center of this complex apparatus lies the individual's biographical core, composed of his civil name, physical body, and natural personality, elements that nonetheless remain almost entirely confined to a shadow zone, acting as an invisible engine that fuels external projections without ever directly exposing itself to the public.

    The primary manifestation of this system is represented by the quasi-subject thasup, previously known as Tha Supreme, whose structural effectiveness lies in the adoption of an avatarial representative module inspired by the aesthetics of 2000s cartoons, which functions as a total interface between the artist and the observer. This figure, characterized by a chromatic palette dominated by purple and by symbolic elements such as horns and a halo, is not simply a graphic illustration, but a technical and replicable iconography that comes to life in music videos, physical installations, and complex digital environments such as the video game UFO JaZZ BoY, guaranteeing absolute systemic coherence between the external appearance and a character mask perceived as mysterious and unreachable.

    The density of the architecture further expands and stratifies through the introduction of an alternate personality identified in the alias yungest Moonstar, a separate path that allows the system to diversify its sonic and behavioral output without compromising the solidity of the main configuration. This splitting operation reveals the deeply flexible nature of Mattei's architecture, where the transition between different aliases and visual identities is managed as a modular reconfiguration of the components of the identity database, allowing the creator to explore new languages while remaining protected behind the screen of his own alter egos.

    The entire structure thus proves to be an architecture based on the rigorous management of visibility and anonymity flows, where the distance between the primary subject and his public manifestations does not constitute a limitation, but rather the vital fulcrum of the system itself, guaranteeing global recognizability precisely through the negation of human physiognomy and the construction of an autonomous narrative universe, perfectly integrated and capable of evolving independently from the physical presence of its author. In this sense, the case of Davide Mattei emerges as an emblematic example of systemic deconstruction and reconstruction of contemporary identity, in which the architecture does not merely serve to inhabit a space, but to build a solid, authorial, and totally shielded digital presence.
